## Approach and Implementation
I read the following papers to help model secure key rate and produce an expected result to compare our real results to: Renner and Wolf (2023) and Islam et al. (2017), Gisin et al. (2002), and Padhye et al. (1998). This led me to a formula for secure key rate as a function of fault parameters.

I presented at the STEM Symposium with new results, showing joint impact of polarization fidelity and detector efficiency, and detector efficiency and packet loss. 

I met with Ashish Khandelwal, a postdoc who works with quantum networks, and Nahar Shlok, a postdoc who specializes in QKD theory. Nahar suggested that the classical network faults would have minimal effects, and gave me more background on QKD theory and security proofs. He suggested papers to read and gaps that our work could go in, such as detector fault study through practical implementation and faults in classical post processing.


## Results
- Created formula for expected key rate given fault parameters
- Presented at STEM Symposium
- Start to shift project direction to focus on faults in classical post-processing, rather than classical network.
